# 01 Investment case and evidence

OfficePress has a coherent product thesis: operational work is spread across conversations, records, files, approvals and transactions, while the buyer wants a process completed. Its four families provide a useful portfolio structure. The investment question is whether a small initial package can deliver that outcome with less adoption effort and sustainable service cost. [P1 P2]


## What makes the case plausible

Integrated business software is an established market. General Atlantic reported an Odoo minority transaction at a EUR7 billion valuation in January 2026 and more than 170000 paying customers. Zoho markets more than 50 apps in its Standard bundle. These are demand and competition signals, not proof that OfficePress has product market fit. [S6 S8]

## The pain hypothesis

A team may already own capable email, spreadsheets and task tools, yet still copy information between them and chase people for the next step. The first discovery interviews should reconstruct a real recent request, identify missed handoffs and measure the time spent. Do not claim a universal hours saved figure without observation.


## A purchase trigger

Sell against a visible operational problem: an unowned enquiry, slow approval, missed follow up or repeated rekeying. Keep existing systems where they work. A controlled first workflow is easier to evaluate than replacing every tool at once.

# 07 Value proposition and defensibility

**Proposed positioning:** OfficePress helps small businesses complete operational work through connected apps they can adopt gradually and control. This is a candidate message for customer testing. It should become a differentiated claim only after the product and commercial offer make it true.

Proposed advantage | Why the buyer might care | Proof required

Incremental adoption | Start with one recurring problem and avoid a forced full suite migration. | A first workflow deployed without replacing unrelated tools.

Company control | Keep meaningful authority over business data and operating choices. | Documented contract, export, recovery and deployment responsibilities.

Connected execution | Reduce handoffs between conversations, records and approvals. | Working identity, permissions, links and state across selected apps.

Consistent experience | Spend less time relearning common actions. | Observed task success across two or more apps.

Contextual agents | Assist within the work rather than in a separate chat context. | Permission checks, review controls, action logs and bounded unit costs.


## What is not yet a moat

App count, a modern interface and AI features are readily matched. Odoo, Zoho, Microsoft and monday already combine broad applications, integrations or AI capabilities. OfficePress should not claim to be the first integrated business suite or the only company controlled option. ERPNext is an explicit self hosting alternative. [S6 S8 S9 S10 S11]


## What could become durable

A defensible business could build repeatable industry workflows, trusted migrations, distribution through capable partners, reliable integrations and a reputation for predictable ownership costs. These assets compound through successful customer operation. A shared technical foundation may lower delivery cost, but savings must be measured after support, upgrades and integration maintenance.


## The falsification test

If target buyers prefer the incumbent bundle after seeing the workflow and full operating cost, narrow the use case or change the package. Differentiation must survive a real purchase decision, not only a feature comparison.

## The assumptions do the filtering

USD1800 per year means USD150 per account per month. The three SAM factors are analyst assumptions, not survey results or independent probabilities. They need replacement with segment counts, technical eligibility and observed purchase behaviour. Applying them sequentially is a transparent planning shortcut, not statistical evidence.


## Sensitivity before confidence

At USD900 to USD3000 annual account revenue, scoped TAM spans about USD8.38bn to USD27.93bn. At the base price, a 1% to 5% serviceability filter yields about USD168m to USD838m of SAM. These ranges show the effect of chosen inputs; they do not reduce uncertainty through additional evidence.

Arithmetic and inputs are saved in research/model.json. One customer is one buying business account, not one seat, app, enterprise establishment or payment transaction. Count each account once; do not add app TAMs or multiply suite revenue by 30.


# 12 Business model options

The company control promise leaves commercial choices open. OfficePress can charge for reliable outcomes, managed operation, supported software or deployment services, but the rights, operating obligations and cost base must be explicit. The following are options, not accepted current offers. [P1]

Model option | Revenue mechanism | Main tradeoff

Managed workflow subscription | Recurring account package, with included users or capacity and transparent usage allowances. | Easy commercial evaluation, but OfficePress owns hosting, support and operational reliability.

Customer controlled deployment | Annual supported software or update subscription; customer or partner operates its environment. | Fits control preference; fragmented versions and support complexity can hurt margins.

Partner delivered suite | Partner implementation and managed services; OfficePress licence, support or platform subscription. | Leverages local expertise but needs training, quality control and channel margin.

Open core or free entry | Free selected capability with paid operation, support or enterprise extensions. | Distribution benefit is possible; licence and conversion design must be chosen deliberately.

Usage and transactions | Meter provider intensive AI, storage or messaging; optional payment or marketplace participation. | Aligns variable costs, but creates predictability, regulatory and dependence concerns.


## Recommended first commercial experiment

Offer one managed workflow package with a defined onboarding scope. Test two recurring price points with comparable prospects, while separately pricing implementation and metered provider usage. For buyers with a clear deployment need, test a supported customer controlled option; do not promise it before operational validation.


## Illustrative packaging for interviews

Use USD79 per account/month as a small entry package, USD149 for the target workflow package and USD299 for a larger operations package, with proposed included users and usage established during interviews. These are test cards, not published prices. The financial model rounds the target package to USD150. Avoid an unlimited all apps promise while the support burden is unknown.


## Revenue quality rules

Separate subscription revenue, one time implementation, partner pass through and variable usage. Only contracted recurring subscription value belongs in the modelled ARR. AI usage should have visible spend limits and measured gross margin; customer infrastructure spend is not automatically OfficePress revenue.

## Where the model can break

A further USD30 of support or provider cost reduces gross profit to USD80, margin to 53.3% and USD1200 CAC payback to 15 months. Low priced accounts with bespoke integrations can destroy otherwise attractive subscription economics. Price setup separately and enforce a supportable scope.


## Measure costs honestly

Include the founder time used for onboarding, presales and customer support in fully loaded delivery or acquisition cost. Report gross retention and expansion separately. Do not claim lifetime value from an unobserved churn rate; the simple revenue scenarios assume 10% annual logo churn only for acquisition planning.

## Revenue is not the exit run rate

A linear average of beginning and ending accounts gives base subscription revenue of USD90000, USD450000 and USD1.44 million in years one, two and three. It excludes setup fees, metered usage and taxes. A monthly cohort and cash collection model is required before a funding decision.


## What the base case requires

At 10% annual logo churn, year three begins with 400 customers, retains 360 and must add 840 to finish at 1200: an average of 70 gross additions per month. A purely sales assisted channel at 20% qualified demo conversion would require 350 qualified demos each month. These assumptions are demanding and must be reconciled with staffing and onboarding capacity.

At USD110 monthly gross profit and USD50000 fixed monthly overhead, approximately 455 active accounts cover fixed overhead before new customer acquisition, onboarding losses, taxes and financing. This is a contribution threshold, not a forecast of company break even.


# 15 Go to market and expansion

**Proposed entry:** one workflow for English speaking service businesses with 5 to 50 people, starting in one or two reachable markets. Global availability is not a substitute for focus. Choose the first geography from founder access, payments, support coverage and customer evidence rather than the largest population alone.

## Sell a workflow that can be demonstrated

A request should become owned work with a next action, a relevant record and a visible result. Use that scenario in discovery, onboarding, product education and competitive testing. The customer should be able to explain why OfficePress remains useful alongside email or accounting software already in place.


## A plausible capacity bridge

One illustrative route to the base year three acquisition rate is 15 productive partners contributing two new accounts per month, plus 40 direct additions. The direct component would need about 200 qualified demos monthly at 20% conversion. This is a workload model to test, not a partner pipeline or a committed sales plan.


## International expansion gates

Before adding markets, verify contracts, tax handling, payments, time zones, support, integrations and language requirements. Accounting, signing, HR and payments can carry market specific obligations; include their validation cost in the roadmap. No certification or jurisdictional compliance claim is established by this briefing.


# 16 Roadmap tied to evidence

The suite vision should guide architecture while customer evidence governs release order. Do not fund 30 simultaneous product launches. The phases below are a proposed operating plan; timing depends on the current team, readiness audit and available cash.

Phase | Deliverable | Proposed evidence gate

First 30 days | Measure the six Shoppable pilots; audit supported scope; interview independent prospects and test two packages. | Pilot usage baseline and outcomes; at least 15 buyer interviews; founder and CEO decision charter.

Days 31 to 90 | Turn pilot learning into a narrow paid package and reproducible onboarding. | Proposed target of five independent paying design partners; measured setup and provider costs.

## Metrics that should govern the programme

Track time to first completed workflow, weekly active buying accounts, recurring work completed, trial to paid conversion, retained paid accounts, gross revenue retention, net revenue retention, support time, provider spend and acquisition payback. Report sample size and cohort age. A team logging in is weaker evidence than a team repeatedly finishing valuable work.


## Stop or narrow conditions

Change the segment or package if discovery does not produce recurring paid problems, pilots require bespoke engineering to become useful, customers do not return to the workflow, or delivery cost exceeds a credible price. A broad catalogue should not prevent closing an unconvincing product experiment.


## Readiness before expansion

For each sold workflow, assign an owner for permissions, backup and restoration, migrations, release support, external provider failures and incident communication. The shared proofs are implementation examples with limits, not an operating SLA for the suite. [P3]
